Scam Alerts

Scams going around right now, based on warnings from government agencies and police. Each alert says what to do and links to the original warning.

PhoneNov 23

Calls from a faked police number asking for passwords

The Brookville (Ohio) Police Department says scammers are making its real phone number show up on caller ID and asking people for passwords, PINs or login details. The department says neither its officers nor its investigators ever call to ask for that kind of information.

What to do: If a caller asks for a password, PIN or login, hang up without sharing anything, even if the caller ID looks right. Call the department's non-emergency line yourself to check or report the call.
Source: WHIO-TV via Yahoo News, reporting the Brookville (Ohio) Police Department warning (Nov 23, 2026)

PhoneBankingNov 22

Fake bank calls that send someone to collect your cards

Kennewick (Wash.) police warn of callers who pose as a bank or credit union, say there is fraud on your account and tell you to cut your debit and credit cards in half. A courier then comes to pick up the cards, and people who handed them over saw them used for fraudulent purchases.

What to do: Hang up and call your bank or credit union at a number you know is real; banks don't send anyone to collect your cards. When you get rid of an old card, destroy it completely, including the chip.
Source: Tri-City Herald via Yahoo News, reporting the Kennewick (Wash.) Police warning (Nov 22, 2026)

PhoneTextNov 20

Anyone demanding payment in gift cards

Police in Swanzey, New Hampshire, report more scams that end with a demand to pay in gift cards. The caller, text, email or social media message may say you owe money, have a warrant, won a prize or must pay an emergency fee, and pushes you to act right away. No real government agency, police department, utility, bank or business asks to be paid with gift cards.

What to do: Never buy gift cards to pay someone who contacted you or share the numbers on the back; hang up and call the organization at a number you know is real. If you already shared the card details, call the gift card company right away and report it.
Source: Newport Dispatch, reporting the Swanzey (N.H.) Police Department warning (Nov 20, 2026)

Search adsHoliday shoppingNov 19

Scam ads at the top of holiday shopping searches

The FTC warns that scammers pay for search ads so their links show up at the top of, or right next to, real results while people shop for the holidays. Clicking one can lead you to pay a scammer or give away details like your bank account number, which can be used to steal your identity.

What to do: Scroll past the paid ads to the regular results, and before buying, search the seller's name with the word "complaint" or "scam". If you already know a store's web address, type it in yourself, and report search scams to the FTC.
Source: FTC: How to avoid holiday shopping scams in your search results (Nov 19, 2026)

WebsiteNov 19

Fake police website that asks for payments

Fairfax County, Va., police say a copycat website is using their department's look and branding. It has links that claim to let you file a police report online and make payments.

What to do: Don't use the copycat site, download files from it or enter personal or payment details there; reach your police department through the county's own official website instead.
Source: DC News Now via Yahoo News, reporting the Fairfax County Police Department warning (Nov 18, 2026)

WebsiteHoliday shoppingNov 18

Fake holiday deal websites built with AI

The FBI's Boston office warns that scammers are using AI to build fake store websites with holiday deals that look too good to be true. It also warns of texts, emails and calls pretending to be from your bank or credit card company that ask for your account number.

What to do: Before you buy, stop and check the store or bank on your own instead of through a link or caller, and report fraud to the FBI at its IC3 complaint site.

TextHoliday shoppingNov 17

Fake package delivery texts before the holidays

The Wayne County (N.Y.) Sheriff's Office warns that holiday shoppers are getting texts saying a package couldn't be delivered or needs a small payment. The links can lead to copycat sites built to collect card numbers and other personal details. The Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department has warned about the same kind of delivery texts, plus fake flash-sale offers ahead of Cyber Monday, and South Dakota's attorney general has also warned shoppers about fake delivery messages.

What to do: Don't tap the link; check your order in the store's own app or website instead, then delete the text or forward it to 7726 to report it.
Source: FingerLakes1.com, reporting the Wayne County (N.Y.) Sheriff's Office warning (Nov 17, 2026) · KTLA, reporting the Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department warning (Nov 16, 2026) · Dakota News Now, reporting the South Dakota Attorney General's warning (Nov 18, 2026)

VeteransPhoneNov 16

Look-alike VA phone numbers and fake veteran mailers

The FTC says scammers are setting up phone numbers that nearly match real VA hotlines, so a single mistyped digit can connect you to someone who charges for services the VA gives free. Others mail postcards for a "Veterans Savings Program" that doesn't exist, hoping you'll call and share your Social Security or bank details, or run social media ads for fake veterans grants.

What to do: Apply for benefits only through the VA's official website or a VA regional office, and double-check any VA number before you call. Accredited helpers don't charge a fee to file a VA claim, so walk away from anyone asking for money up front.
Source: FTC: Three new tactics scammers use to go after VA benefits (Nov 16, 2026)

ComputerPhoneNov 15

Fake virus pop-ups that tell you to call for help

The Fort Bend County (Texas) Sheriff's Office is warning about alarming pop-ups and fake security alerts that say your computer was hacked, infected or found to hold illegal material. The message gives a phone number that claims to be Microsoft or another tech support company, and the person who answers demands payment with gift cards, cryptocurrency or cash fed into a Bitcoin ATM.

What to do: Don't call a number shown in a surprise pop-up, and never let someone who contacted you take remote control of your computer. Real tech companies don't ask to be paid with gift cards or Bitcoin ATM deposits.
Source: KPRC Click2Houston, reporting the Fort Bend County Sheriff's Office warning (Nov 15, 2026)

OnlinePayment appsNov 14

Marketplace buyers who ask you to send money back

The Jacksonville Sheriff's Office says a Facebook Marketplace seller lost $700 after a "buyer" claimed their business Zelle account could only send a larger amount, asked the seller to return the difference, and no payment ever arrived. The scammer then sent a fake customer service link and talked the seller into paying for a supposed account upgrade.

What to do: Check that the money is really in your account before you hand over an item or send anything back, because a real buyer will never ask you to refund part of a payment that hasn't cleared. Report the attempt to your local police.
Source: News4JAX, reporting the Jacksonville Sheriff's Office warning (Nov 14, 2026)

EmailSmall businessNov 13

Fake city emails asking for permit fees

The City of Springfield, Oregon, says a scammer posing as a Planning Commission member emailed someone with a pending development application, used public details about the project, and attached an invoice to be paid by wire transfer. The FBI has warned that this scheme is happening nationwide, with fake invoices that threaten permit delays and ask for wire, payment app or cryptocurrency payments.

What to do: Before paying any permit or application fee, call the city or county office using the number on its official website, not one in the email. If you already paid, contact your bank right away and report it to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center.
Source: KMTR NBC16, reporting the City of Springfield (Ore.) warning (Nov 13, 2026) · FBI IC3 Public Service Announcement I-030926-PSA

PhoneNov 13

Calls claiming your card bought a gun

Quincy, Illinois, police say callers pretending to be a credit card company's fraud team claim your card was used to buy a firearm in another city. The charge never happened, and some people called don't even have a card from that company. The goal is to get personal details or card numbers, and in one case the caller had the person download Signal to talk to a fake FBI agent.

What to do: Hang up and call the number on the back of your card to check for any fraud alert. Don't give personal information to someone who called you, and know that the FBI won't contact you through Signal.
Source: Quincy Herald-Whig, reporting the Quincy (Ill.) Police Department warning (Nov 13, 2026)

WebsitesPhoneNov 12

Fake FTC websites and FTC impersonators

The FTC says scammers copy its name, logo and page design to build look-alike websites, and contact people by phone, text, email or social media claiming to be FTC agents, sometimes with fake badges. Some tell you to move your money or hand over cash to keep it safe.

What to do: Before typing in personal details, check that the site's address ends in .gov, since real FTC sites always do. A real FTC employee will never ask you to move money or hand cash to anyone.
Source: FTC: How to spot fake FTC websites and FTC impersonators

PhoneNov 12

Calls claiming your child was hurt and needs money

Norman, Oklahoma, police say callers are telling parents their child was in a crash or an assault and was injured, then asking for money for medical care or bail. Someone may come on the line pretending to be the child, blaming an injury for sounding different, and the caller may say police took the child's phone. They push for fast payment by gift card, payment app or wire transfer. The FTC has also warned about the same kind of call aimed at grandparents, and says the voice or even a video of a loved one can be faked. The Pima County, Arizona, sheriff's department and the FBI have also warned that scammers use AI to copy a relative's voice.

What to do: Hang up and call your child yourself, then contact your local police. Never pay with gift cards or give personal details to someone who called you.

OnlineNov 12

Illegal online pharmacies selling unsafe medicine

The Georgia Attorney General's Office warns that some online pharmacies are illegal and may sell counterfeit, expired, unapproved or poorly stored drugs that can harm your health. Warning signs include selling prescription drugs without a valid prescription, prices that look too low, and no checkable U.S. address or licensed pharmacist.

What to do: Before ordering, confirm the pharmacy is licensed through your state board of pharmacy and ask a licensed pharmacist if you have questions. Report a suspicious pharmacy to your state attorney general.
Source: WSB-TV via Yahoo News, reporting the Georgia Attorney General's Office alert (Nov 12, 2026)

Social mediaEmailNov 12

Fake charities asking for holiday donations

Graham, North Carolina, police warn that scammers set up fake charities or pose as real ones during the holiday giving season, reaching people by social media, email, text or phone. They use emotional stories, urgent appeals and look-alike fundraising pages, and may ask for gift cards, cryptocurrency or transfers to personal accounts.

What to do: Research a charity with a charity rating service before giving, and donate through its official website rather than a link in a message you did not expect. Report a fake charity to your local police or the FTC.
Source: WXLV ABC45, reporting the Graham (N.C.) Police Department warning (Nov 12, 2026)

OnlineNov 11

Fake rental listings on real estate websites

North Canton, Ohio, police say scammers are posting fake rental listings on well-known real estate websites. Renters are asked to fill out an application with pay stubs and bank records, then to send a security deposit or other payment by bank transfer, which can lead to lost money and identity theft. Police note that a listing on a familiar site is not proof it is real.

What to do: Confirm who owns the property and that the person is allowed to rent it before sharing financial documents or paying any deposit. If you shared information or sent money, contact your local police.
Source: Cleveland19, reporting the North Canton (Ohio) Police Department warning (Nov 11, 2026)

Calls asking church members to buy gift cards

Police in Ripon, Wisconsin, say callers contacted members of a local church and asked them to buy gift cards, claiming the money would help another member with medical needs. VeteransSocial mediaNov 10

Fake VA grant offers sent on social media

The FTC warns that scammers are mining veterans' social media profiles, such as service history, promotion photos and tagged places, then messaging them about grants while claiming to be from the Department of Veterans Affairs. The messages lead to convincing fake sites that ask for a Social Security number or VA account login. Fake profiles using military lingo are also pushing "guaranteed" investment schemes aimed at veterans.

What to do: The VA won't message you on social media, so check any benefit offer by going to the VA's official website yourself, and never give your Social Security number or VA login to someone who contacted you. Don't take investment advice from someone you met online.
Source: FTC: A Veterans Day reminder on how to spot scams on social media

PhoneNov 10

Callers posing as deputies or police threaten arrest

Sheriff's offices and police departments are warning about callers who pretend to be deputies or officers, say you have a warrant or missed jury duty, and threaten arrest unless money is sent, sometimes as Bitcoin or a prepaid "bond." Some use a real officer's name or fake the caller ID. Police in Texas, North Carolina, South Carolina, Connecticut, Pennsylvania and New Jersey have warned about this, and all say officers never demand payment by phone to clear a warrant.

What to do: Hang up, don't send money, gift cards or cryptocurrency, and call the department at a number you look up yourself, not one the caller gives you.

PhoneNov 10

Fake jail calls asking for a fee to free a relative

Court officials and the sheriff's office in Boone County, Missouri, say scammers are using public jail rosters to find people in custody, then calling their family and friends while pretending to be jail, police or court staff. The callers say the person can go home on electronic monitoring once an upfront fee for an ankle device is paid, sometimes naming real judges and booking details, and press for quick payment by payment app, cryptocurrency or gift card. Police in Richland, Washington, have warned about a similar version that sends official-looking court papers with electronic monitoring prices and asks for payment by Chime barcode.

What to do: Hang up and check on the person by calling the jail or court at a number you look up yourself. Court officials don't call asking for money and don't accept gift cards, payment apps or wire transfers.
Source: KBIA, reporting warnings from Boone County, Mo., court officials and the Boone County Sheriff's Office (Nov 10, 2026) · Tri-City Herald via Yahoo News, reporting the Richland (Wash.) Police warning (Nov 22, 2026)

PhoneBankingNov 9

Fake bank calls telling you to hand cash to a courier

Police warn of callers who pose as your bank, police or a lawyer, claim there is an urgent problem with your money, and tell you to withdraw cash and hand it to a courier or someone who comes to pick it up, sometimes an unaware rideshare driver. Callers tell you to stay on the line and keep it secret, and the calls can be disguised to look like they come from the real bank. Police in Morristown and Egg Harbor Township, N.J., and police and the FBI in Las Vegas have warned about this.

What to do: No real bank or police department will ask you to withdraw cash and hand it to someone, so hang up and call your bank using the number on the back of your card. Never give a caller your passwords, PINs or one-time codes.
Source: WRNJ Radio, reporting the Morristown Police Department warning (Nov 9, 2026) · 100.1 WJRZ, reporting the Egg Harbor Township (N.J.) Police Department warning (Nov 14, 2026) · 8 News Now via Yahoo News, reporting the Las Vegas Metropolitan Police Department warning (Nov 17, 2026) · 96.3 KKLZ, reporting the Las Vegas Metropolitan Police Department and FBI Las Vegas warning (Nov 20, 2026)

Fake FBI agents calling from real FBI numbers

FBI Honolulu warned that scammers are posing as FBI, DEA and Secret Service agents and faking the phone numbers of real FBI offices, so calling back the number can still reach the scammer. Victims are threatened with arrest and told to pay. In one case, the scammer used a video call with a background made to look like a federal office.

What to do: Hang up, look up the agency's number yourself, and never pay anyone who says they're a government officer, since real agencies don't demand payment by phone, prepaid card, cryptocurrency or courier.

PhoneOct 22

Fake FTC calls threaten to shut off your phone

The FTC warned that callers posing as FTC officials claim a phone number in your name was used for scams in another country and threaten to disconnect your phone. They transfer you to a fake police officer, tell you to keep the call secret, then claim you're under arrest and demand a "security deposit."

What to do: Hang up. Real FTC employees never ask for money, threaten to cut off your phone or tell you to keep a call secret. Report the call to the FTC.

DonationsTextOct 15

Fake campaign "investments" and scam PACs

The FBI warned that scammers are using the midterm elections to run fake campaign investment pools, pose as political action committees, sell merchandise that never ships, and send fake voter registration messages. Some use AI-generated voices of candidates.

What to do: Donations are never investments. Check a committee on the Federal Election Commission's website before giving, and confirm your registration on the official federal voter registration site.
Source: FBI IC3 Public Service Announcement I-101526-3-PSA

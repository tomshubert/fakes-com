Scam Alerts

Scams going around right now, based on warnings from government agencies and police. Each alert says what to do and links to the original warning.

PhoneNov 10

Fake jail calls asking for a fee to free a relative

Court officials and the sheriff's office in Boone County, Missouri, say scammers are using public jail rosters to find people in custody, then calling their family and friends while pretending to be jail, police or court staff. The callers say the person can go home on electronic monitoring once an upfront fee for an ankle device is paid, sometimes naming real judges and booking details, and press for quick payment by payment app, cryptocurrency or gift card.

What to do: Hang up and check on the person by calling the jail or court at a number you look up yourself. Court officials don't call asking for money and don't accept gift cards, payment apps or wire transfers.
Source: KBIA, reporting warnings from Boone County, Mo., court officials and the Boone County Sheriff's Office (Nov 10, 2026)
PhoneBankingNov 9

Fake bank calls telling you to hand cash to a courier

Police in Morristown, New Jersey, warn of callers who pose as your bank, claim there is suspicious activity on your account, and tell you to withdraw a large amount of cash and give it to a courier or other person. The calls can be disguised to look like they come from the real bank, and some callers say you are helping with a secret "sting."

What to do: No real bank or police department will ask you to withdraw cash and hand it to someone, so hang up and call your bank using the number on the back of your card. Never give a caller your passwords, PINs or one-time codes.
Source: WRNJ Radio, reporting the Morristown Police Department warning (Nov 9, 2026)
VeteransPhoneNov 9

Scams aimed at service members and veterans

New York's attorney general published a guide to scams that target the military community, including people who charge veterans to "help" with VA claims, offers of quick cash in exchange for pension payments, overpriced financing sold per pay period, and fake charities.

What to do: Don't pay anyone to help file your first VA claim, and check any benefits message by logging in to your official account directly.
Source: New York Attorney General: Scams targeting the military · Yahoo News, reporting the NY Attorney General's new guide (Nov 9, 2026)
PhoneBusinessNov 7

Callers pressuring employees to use Bitcoin ATMs

Police in Ocean City, Md., say scammers are calling hotels, complimenting staff and offering a "bonus" for settling a supposed balance with cash from the register or safe through a Bitcoin ATM.

What to do: Tell staff that the business owner will never ask them to pay a balance with cash from the register or safe through a Bitcoin ATM. Report any such call to police.
Source: CoastTV, reporting the Ocean City Police Department warning
PhoneVideo callNov 2

Fake FBI agents calling from real FBI numbers

FBI Honolulu warned that scammers are posing as FBI, DEA and Secret Service agents and faking the phone numbers of real FBI offices, so calling back the number can still reach the scammer. Victims are threatened with arrest and told to pay. In one case, the scammer used a video call with a background made to look like a federal office.

What to do: Hang up, look up the agency's number yourself, and never pay anyone who says they're a government officer, since real agencies don't demand payment by phone, prepaid card, cryptocurrency or courier.
Source: Maui Now, reporting the FBI Honolulu warning (Nov 2, 2026) · FBI IC3 Public Service Announcement I-091726-PSA
PhoneOct 22

Fake FTC calls threaten to shut off your phone

The FTC warned that callers posing as FTC officials claim a phone number in your name was used for scams in another country and threaten to disconnect your phone. They transfer you to a fake police officer, tell you to keep the call secret, then claim you're under arrest and demand a "security deposit."

What to do: Hang up. Real FTC employees never ask for money, threaten to cut off your phone or tell you to keep a call secret. Report the call to the FTC.
Source: FTC: The FTC is not calling to deactivate your phone number
DonationsTextOct 15

Fake campaign "investments" and scam PACs

The FBI warned that scammers are using the midterm elections to run fake campaign investment pools, pose as political action committees, sell merchandise that never ships, and send fake voter registration messages. Some use AI-generated voices of candidates.

What to do: Donations are never investments. Check a committee on the Federal Election Commission's website before giving, and confirm your registration on the official federal voter registration site.
Source: FBI IC3 Public Service Announcement I-101526-3-PSA

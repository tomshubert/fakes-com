Cheap charging cables are everywhere, and many are counterfeit or uncertified. Apple warns that these can damage your device, wear out quickly, overheat, fit poorly, or fail to charge. This guide covers Lightning cables and adapters, the ones Apple's official guidance addresses.

Look for Apple's MFi badge

Apple certifies third-party accessories through its MFi (Made for iPhone, iPad and iPod) program. Certified accessories carry the MFi badge on their packaging; some older ones show an earlier version of the badge. A cable sold with no packaging, or without the badge, hasn't shown you it's certified.

Check the cable itself

For Apple's own Lightning to USB cable, Apple says to look about seven inches from the USB end for the printed text "Designed by Apple in California" followed by where it was assembled (China, Vietnam or Brazil) and a 12-digit serial number.

The Lightning end (plugs into your iPhone)

  • Genuine: one solid piece, with smooth, rounded gold or silver contacts.
  • Warning signs: a connector made of several pieces, rough or squared-off contacts, or a white or black faceplate instead of a gray or metallic one.

The USB end

  • Genuine: gold-plated contacts and a smooth, flat metal shell.
  • Warning signs: silver-colored contacts, a grainy surface, or notches in the plastic insert.

If a cable gets hot, stop using it

Apple lists overheating among the problems counterfeit accessories can cause. Don't charge with a suspect cable overnight or near anything flammable.

Where to buy safely

Buy from Apple, a store listed on Apple's location finder, or a reputable brand that shows the MFi badge. Very cheap cables from unknown marketplace sellers carry the most risk.

Free steps that stop the most trouble

  1. Freeze your credit. The FTC says a credit freeze is free to place and to lift, and it recommends one even if nothing has gone wrong yet. You set it up with each of the three credit bureaus: Equifax, Experian and TransUnion. It generally stops new credit from being opened in your name, and lifting it when you apply for credit yourself is free too.
  2. Turn on two-step verification for your email, bank and phone carrier accounts, so a stolen password alone isn't enough to get in.
  3. Use a different password for every account. A password manager makes that easy. Bitwarden has a free version.
  4. Turn on spam filtering for calls and texts in your phone's settings or your carrier's app, and forward scam texts to 7726.
  5. Get off people-search sites. These sites sell your address, phone number and relatives' names, which scammers use to sound convincing. You can ask each site to remove you for free; in a Consumer Reports test, people who did their own opt-outs got more of their listings removed than any paid service did. If you live in California, the state's free DROP tool sends one deletion request to more than 600 registered data brokers.
  6. Check before you click. If a message, link or offer seems off, paste it into our checker.

If something has already happened

  • Call your bank or card company right away if you sent money or shared card details.
  • Report scams at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and identity theft at IdentityTheft.gov for a step-by-step recovery plan.

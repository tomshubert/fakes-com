I built Fakes.com because, in my work, I've watched good, careful people lose real money to scams that looked completely real: phishing emails that stole their logins, and "urgent" requests to wire money that appeared to come from their own boss.

I'm an IT consultant. I support about 100 businesses and individuals, mostly companies with about 50 users or fewer, with everything from their computers and networks to their email security. A big part of that job now is stopping scams before they work: a fake invoice, a phony login page, a call from "the bank." This site is the advice I give my clients, written for everyone.

My background

  • I started working with computers in 8th grade, at 13.
  • After graduating from Emory University, I began consulting in 1982 as a contract programmer on mainframe and minicomputer projects for Atlanta companies, including special projects at The Coca-Cola Company's headquarters.
  • In 1984 a partner and I founded a microcomputer consulting company that built PCs and set up and supported office networks. It became a Novell Direct Gold Reseller.
  • In 1988 I moved from Atlanta to Los Angeles and went in-house, running the network and computer systems for a securities firm.
  • By 1994 I had returned to independent IT consulting for small businesses, and I also started a web services business, YourWEB.

Today I offer clients 24/7 Microsoft 365 account and identity monitoring through a security operations center. When it spots something suspicious, like a sign-in from another country, I'm alerted right away and can lock the account.

Someone in Germany signed into the email account of an employee at a law firm I support. The monitoring service flagged the login right away, and I locked the account down before any sensitive firm or client data was compromised.

Other sites I trust

Each of these is free to use and doesn't take sides.

If you've been scammed

  • ReportFraud.ftc.gov: the Federal Trade Commission's site for reporting scams and other consumer problems.
  • IC3.gov: the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center, for online fraud, hacked accounts, identity theft and online extortion.
  • AARP Fraud Watch Network Helpline: free support and guidance if you've been targeted by a scam. Call 877-908-3360, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Eastern.

Check for yourself

  • Have I Been Pwned: shows whether your email address turned up in a known data breach.
  • FactCheck.org: a nonpartisan, nonprofit fact-checker run by the Annenberg Public Policy Center at the University of Pennsylvania.
  • DataCenterDialog: sourced, dated facts about data centers, including power, water, jobs, taxes and noise, with each side's strongest case shown side by side.

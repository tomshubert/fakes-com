"Your package couldn't be delivered." "You have an unpaid toll." "Did you just spend $499? Reply YES or NO." Texts like these are some of the most common scams going.

Scam texts work because they're short, they look official and they arrive on the device you carry everywhere. One tap on a link can take you to a copycat website built to steal your card number or passwords.

The numbers behind it

The FTC says people reported losing $470 million to scams that started with a text message in 2024.

The five text scams the FTC sees most

  • Fake delivery problems. A package "couldn't be delivered" or needs a small fee or your address. Police in Los Angeles County and New York warned about a surge of these before the holidays.
  • Phony job offers. Offers of easy work or quick pay that turn out to be scams.
  • Fake fraud alerts. A message that looks like it's from your bank, asking whether you made a purchase.
  • Unpaid tolls. Bogus notices that you owe money for a toll.
  • "Wrong number" texts. A stranger who seems to have texted the wrong person. The FTC says these aren't really mistakes.

Warning signs

The FTC says to be suspicious of any text you didn't expect that:

  • Asks for personal or financial details, such as passwords, account numbers or your Social Security number.
  • Says there's a problem with your account, a payment, a purchase or a delivery.
  • Promises prizes, gift cards, coupons or help with loans.
  • Includes a link, especially one that leads to a site that looks like a company you know.

The FTC puts it simply: real companies won't ask for your account information by text.

What to do

  1. Don't tap the link and don't reply, not even "STOP."
  2. Check it yourself. If the message might be real, go to the company's own app or website, or call a number you already know is real. Track packages on the store's or carrier's site.
  3. Report it. Forward the text to 7726 (it spells SPAM) so your phone company can block similar messages, and use "Report junk" in your messaging app.
  4. Turn on spam filtering in your phone's settings or your carrier's app.

If you already tapped or paid

  1. If you paid or shared card details, call your bank or card company right away.
  2. If you typed in a password, change it, and turn on two-step verification.
  3. Report it to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

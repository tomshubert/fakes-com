Fake photos, AI videos and false rumors going around now, each checked by an established fact-checker. Tap any card to see the fake, safely labeled, at the fact-checker's site.

Fake Mislabeled map · Nov 24

Map of lead fallout from the Iowa Army plant blast

A map shared on X claimed to show where lead dust would fall after the Army's planned detonation at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant. Lead Stories found it was a snowfall forecast from a college weather-model site and matched neither the Army's maps nor one AP published.

The clue: The map's own title says it shows snowfall, and NAM is a NOAA weather model, not a military one.

Fake AI video · Nov 23

Huge waves flooding Gordon's Bay, South Africa

A video shared on Threads claimed to show powerful waves surging over the beachfront at Gordon's Bay, South Africa. Lead Stories found it was made with AI, and an earlier TikTok copy carried an AI-generated label.

The clue: Look closely: cars sit on top of houses, no people are around, and the flooded buildings show no damage.

Fake AI video · Nov 23

Giant sea worm rising behind a cruise ship

A video on X claimed to show an enormous worm-like creature rising from the ocean behind a cruise ship. Lead Stories found AI detection tools rated it very likely AI-made, and no news outlet reported any such sighting.

The clue: The creature looks like the sandworm from the movie Dune, and a news search turns up no reports of it.

Fake AI photo · Nov 20

Malcolm in the Middle stars buying GTA in 2013 and 2026

Two photos shared on X claimed to show actors Bryan Cranston and Erik Per Sullivan buying Grand Theft Auto in 2013 and again buying GTA VI in 2026. Lead Stories found both pictures were made with AI, and detection tools found Google's AI watermark in them.

The clue: Check the ages: Sullivan was born in 1991, yet the 2013 picture shows a child and the 2026 one shows a teenager.

Fake AI video · Nov 19

Woman on a treadmill falling out a gym window

A TikTok video presented as new showed a woman on a malfunctioning treadmill falling out of a gym window, with the gym ordered to pay a large fine. Lead Stories found it was made with AI from one frame of security footage of a real 2024 accident in Indonesia, and the details in the narration don't match the real case.

The clue: Look at the background: some people in the clip are frozen like equipment, and a treadmill shakes while nothing above it moves.

Fake AI image · Nov 18

Government memo warning about an iPhone update

An image shared online claimed to be a Homeland Security Investigations memo saying an iOS 27 update secretly listens to nearby conversations and sends them to Apple for ads. Lead Stories found the memo was not real, and several AI detectors flagged the image as AI-made.

The clue: A scary official-looking memo that only shows up as a screenshot, with no agency or Apple announcement behind it, deserves doubt.

Fake Fake letter · Nov 17

Rockstar letter delaying GTA VI to 2027

An Instagram post shared what looked like a Rockstar Games letter saying Grand Theft Auto VI was pushed back a full year to November 2027. Lead Stories found no such announcement from Rockstar or any major news outlet, and the post itself was tagged as ragebait.

The clue: A delay this big would be on the company's own news page and everywhere in the news, not just one social post.

Fake Edited photo · Nov 17

Sabrina Carpenter and co-stars in a Palantir ad

A post claimed a photo of Sabrina Carpenter, Margaret Qualley and Madelyn Cline was an ad for the tech company Palantir. Lead Stories found it was a behind-the-scenes still from a Carpenter music video, edited to add Palantir branding, and posted by an account that calls itself satire.

The clue: Check the posting account's bio: this one says all its posts are satire.

Fake False rumor · Nov 16

A nationwide 90-day blackout starting Nov. 17

TikTok posts, one using an edited clip of the president, claimed a 90-day nationwide blackout would start Nov. 17. PolitiFact rated it false: the clip was doctored, and what is actually planned that day is a national Emergency Alert System test on TV and radio.

The clue: No agency announced a blackout, and urgent warnings with no named official source are a red flag.

Fake False rumor · Nov 16

King Charles's "urgent address" about his son

A website story claimed King Charles III gave a tearful emergency address about a tragedy involving his son, with a photo of him crying. Lead Stories found no such speech on the royal family's site or in the news, and detection tools flagged the photo as AI-made.

The clue: Read past the headline: the story's own text admits there's no verified evidence the address happened.

Fake AI video · Nov 14

Travelers sleeping in a U.S. airport during the ground stop

A viral clip was shared as showing crowds sleeping in an American airport during the nationwide FAA ground stop on Nov. 13. Lead Stories found the video was most likely made with AI and is not real footage.

The clue: Look for odd details: piles of bedroom pillows, two identical women in matching pink hoodies, bodies blending together, and soda-brand posters announcing cancellations.

Fake False rumor · Nov 12

Jensen Huang quote about "focusing on women"

A meme quoted Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang saying smart people don't waste time focusing on women. Lead Stories found no evidence he ever said it and no news coverage of the remark.

The clue: The quote names no source, date or event, and no news outlet reported it.

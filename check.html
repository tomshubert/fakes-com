A picture of an email can't show where its links really go. The words and the sender's name can look exactly right while the buttons lead to a scam website. For a full check, save the email as a file and add it instead (see below). Until then, don't click any links in it.

How to save a suspicious email as a file

A saved email (.eml file) includes hidden details that show whether it really came from the company it claims. Don't click anything inside the email.

  • Gmail (on a computer): open the email, click the three dots at the top right, then Download message.
  • Outlook (on a computer): drag the email from your inbox onto your desktop, or open it and choose File → Save As.
  • Apple Mail: select the email, then File → Save As, and choose Raw Message Source.

Got an email attachment you're not sure about?

Add it above without opening it. We read PDFs, Word and Excel files, and HTML attachments (a common trick that opens a fake login page). Programs, compressed files and other risky types are never uploaded; we warn you right away based on the file's name.

Fakes.com doesn't scan files for viruses. For that, the free service VirusTotal checks a file with dozens of antivirus programs. Files uploaded there can be seen by security researchers, so don't upload private documents.

Already sent money or shared a password?

Call your bank or card company now using the number on your card or statement, change the password, and report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov.

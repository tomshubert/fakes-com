How to Check an Online Store Before You Pay

Fake shops look polished and often advertise on social media. A one-minute check tells you whether to trust them.

By Tom Shubert · Updated Nov 9, 2026

Fake online stores are some of the best-looking scams on the internet. They copy real brands' photos, run ads on social media, and offer prices that feel like a lucky find. Then the product never arrives, or what shows up is a cheap knockoff.

Where these scams start

According to the FTC, shopping scams were the most-reported type of scam that started on social media in 2025, and more than 40% of people who lost money to a scam on social media said they ordered something they saw in an ad there.

The one-minute check

  1. Search the store's name plus "scam," "complaint" or "review." The FTC recommends this first. Do the same with the exact web address. If other people got burned, they usually say so.
  2. Look at the web address carefully. Fake stores use addresses that look close to real ones, with an extra word, a dash, or an unusual ending. If you got there from an ad, go to the brand's real site by typing it yourself.
  3. Look for real contact information. A legitimate store lists a physical address and a way to reach a person. U.S. Customs and Border Protection has long listed missing contact details as a warning sign.
  4. Compare the price. If everything on the site is on sale, or the price is far below every major retailer, treat that as a serious warning sign.
  5. Watch what happens at checkout. Being sent to an unfamiliar payment page is another red flag CBP warns about.

How you pay matters most

Payment methodIf something goes wrong
Credit cardBest protection. You can dispute charges for items that never came, were wrong, or were damaged.
Debit cardProtections vary by bank, and the money is out of your account while a dispute is reviewed.
Payment apps, wire transfer, gift cards, cryptocurrencyUsually gone for good. The FTC says a seller who will only take these is probably a scammer.

Tom's advice

Keep one credit card just for online shopping, with a low limit. If a site turns out to be fake, you dispute one charge and replace one card, and your main accounts are never exposed.

Keep your receipts

Save the order confirmation, the receipt and screenshots of the product page. Under FTC rules, sellers have to ship when they promise or give you the chance to cancel for a refund, and you'll need proof if you have to dispute a charge.

If you've been scammed

  1. Call your credit card company and dispute the charge.
  2. Report the store to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
  3. Report the ad to the social media platform where you saw it.

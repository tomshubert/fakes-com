Black Friday and Holiday Shopping Scams: How to Shop Safely

Fake deals, copycat stores, delivery texts and gift card demands. What the FBI and FTC warn about every holiday season, and the habits that keep your money safe.

By Tom Shubert · Updated Nov 17, 2026

The holiday shopping season is also scam season. The FBI and FTC warn every year about fake sales, copycat stores and "problem with your delivery" messages aimed at people hunting for deals.

The numbers behind it

The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center counted more than $785 million in losses in 2024 from scams where people paid for things that never arrived, or sent goods and never got paid.

The scams to watch for

  • Deals that are too good to be true. Big discounts on brand-name products, often on a website you've never heard of. The FBI's advice is blunt: if a deal looks too good to be true, it probably is.
  • Social media ads from fake stores. The FTC warns that scammers advertise heavily on social media, sometimes pretending to be well-known brands.
  • Items that never arrive. You pay, and nothing ships. The FBI calls this non-delivery fraud.
  • Fake delivery and flash-sale texts. Messages about a package problem, a redelivery fee or a limited-time sale, with a link. The Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department warned about exactly these ahead of Cyber Monday.
  • Gift card and wire transfer demands. The FBI and FTC both say a seller who insists on gift cards, wire transfers, payment apps or cryptocurrency is a warning sign.

Five habits that protect you

  1. Look up the seller first. The FTC suggests searching the store's name and web address with words like "review," "complaint" or "scam" before you buy.
  2. Go to the store yourself. Type the retailer's address or open its app instead of tapping links in ads, emails or texts. Track packages only through the store or carrier.
  3. Pay with a credit card. The FTC says credit cards let you dispute charges for items that never come or arrive wrong or damaged.
  4. Keep your receipts and order numbers, and check your card statements during and after the holidays.
  5. Use a different password for every store account, as the FBI advises.

If you've been scammed

  1. Call your bank or card company right away and ask to dispute the charge.
  2. Report it to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and to the FBI at IC3.gov.

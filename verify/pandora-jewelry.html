How to Tell if Pandora Jewelry Is Real

Fake Pandora charms and bracelets are widely sold online. Here's what Pandora itself says to check.

By Tom Shubert · Updated Nov 11, 2026

Pandora charms and bracelets are a popular target for copycats, and most fakes are sold online. Pandora publishes its own brand protection guidance. Here's what it says, in plain English.

Buy from the right places

Pandora says it sells through more than 10,300 authorized retailers around the world, and that buying from one of them is the way to be sure you're getting the real thing. Some of those retailers also sell online through their own web shops. Pandora's website has a store locator for finding an authorized retailer near you, and Pandora runs its own official online store in some countries.

Two points Pandora makes clearly on its brand protection page:

  • Auction sites aren't authorized. Pandora says it doesn't allow any retailer to sell its jewelry on internet auction sites. Buying there, including pieces resold from someone's personal collection, is at your own risk.
  • No warranty from unauthorized sellers. Pandora says products bought from sellers that aren't authorized aren't covered by its warranty, and that such sellers rarely offer customer service either.

Signs of a fake Pandora website

Pandora admits it can be hard to tell whether a site is legitimate. It says copycat sites may:

  • Put "Pandora" in the site's name
  • Use copies of Pandora's own product photos
  • Link to Pandora's official website so they look connected to it
  • Look poorly designed, or show low-quality or wrong images

If you're unsure about a shop, check it against Pandora's store locator before you pay.

Check the markings on the jewelry

Pandora describes two stamps found on its pieces:

  • The maker's mark "ALE". Pandora says this appears on almost all of its jewelry, though some of the smallest pieces don't carry it. It stands for Algot Enevoldsen, the father of Pandora's founder.
  • A metal quality mark. Pandora says sterling silver is stamped S925, 14-carat gold is stamped G585 and 18-carat gold is stamped G750. Pieces made before June 2011 show only the numbers, without the letter.

Pandora warns that these marks are not a guarantee, because counterfeit copies can carry similar stamps. A missing mark is a reason to doubt a piece, but a correct-looking stamp doesn't prove it's real. Buying from an authorized retailer is the check Pandora relies on.

If you think you have a fake

  • Report it to Pandora. Pandora asks people to email its Brand Protection team (the address is on its brand protection page) with a link to the seller and details of the suspected fake, written in English. It wants to hear about sellers on auction sites, websites, market stalls, shops and printed ads.
  • Ask for your money back. The FTC says to ask the seller for a refund first, and if that fails, to dispute the charge with your payment provider. It notes that credit cards give you the most protection. Keep your receipts and order emails, since they help with a dispute.
  • Report the seller to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov if you think you were scammed.

How to Tell if Lululemon Is Real

Fake lululemon gear is often sold through copycat websites and auction listings. Here's what lululemon itself says to watch for.

By Tom Shubert · Updated Nov 18, 2026

Lululemon leggings and jackets are popular with counterfeiters, and much of the fake gear is sold through websites made to look like the real store. Lululemon doesn't offer a serial number or code you can look up, so the best protection is knowing where the genuine product is sold and how the copycat sites give themselves away. Here's what lululemon says, in plain English.

Where to buy

Lululemon says its genuine products are sold only in its own stores, on its registered websites and through retailers it has approved. It warns that if you buy anywhere else, you may not be getting the real thing.

In the U.S., lululemon's own online store is shop.lululemon.com. Its brand protection page lists its official websites in other countries too, so you can compare any site you find against that list. A site that isn't on it, isn't one of lululemon's stores and isn't a retailer you know to be approved deserves extra caution.

What lululemon offers to verify a product

Lululemon's guidance is about checking the seller, not the item. It doesn't publish a product lookup or a list of features to inspect. So the checks below can raise or lower your confidence, but none of them can prove a piece of clothing is genuine.

Signs of a fake lululemon website

Lululemon lists these warning signs:

  • The web address. Counterfeit sites often put "lululemon," or a shortened version such as "lulu" or "lulus," in their address and claim to be authorized sellers.
  • A copied look. Fake sites often copy the design and layout of lululemon's real site, so a familiar look is not a reason to trust it.
  • Odd links. Links that lead to other brands' websites, auction sites or blogs.
  • Contact details that don't add up. Contact details or email addresses that don't look like they belong to the company, such as a lululemon-branded address at a free email service.

Lululemon also says it works to take down unauthorized websites and auction listings, so treat auction and marketplace listings with the same care.

A real-looking site isn't proof

Because copycat sites borrow lululemon's photos and layout, the site looking right tells you very little. Check the web address against lululemon's own list before you enter any card details.

If you think you bought a fake

  1. Tell lululemon. It asks shoppers who suspect a store or website of selling counterfeit lululemon products to report it through its contact page, and says it investigates the leads it receives.
  2. Keep your records: the listing, the receipt and any messages with the seller.
  3. If you paid by credit card, contact your card company about disputing the charge. The FTC recommends paying for online orders by credit card for this reason.
  4. Report the seller to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

U.S. Customs and Border Protection gives similar advice for any brand: buy from retailers and online sellers with a good reputation, because many counterfeits arrive in small packages ordered online.

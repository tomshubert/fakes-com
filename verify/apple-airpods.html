How to Tell if Your AirPods Are Real

AirPods are a common target for counterfeiters. Here's how to check yours using Apple's own tools.

By Tom Shubert · Updated Nov 9, 2026

AirPods are a common target for counterfeiters, and fakes can look very close to the real thing in photos. The good news is that Apple lets you look up a serial number yourself.

Step 1: Find the serial number

Apple lists several places to find it:

  • On your iPhone or iPad: open Settings, tap Bluetooth, tap your AirPods' name, then tap the More Info button.
  • On the case: for AirPods and AirPods Pro, it's on the underside of the charging case lid.
  • On AirPods Max: remove the left ear cushion.
  • On the box or receipt: next to the barcode on the original packaging, or on your receipt.

The number in Settings should match the one on the case lid, the box and the receipt. Each earbud also has its own, different serial number, so don't compare those. If the case or box number doesn't match, ask the seller why.

Step 2: Check it with Apple

Enter the serial number on Apple's Check Coverage site at checkcoverage.apple.com. For a serial number Apple recognizes, it shows the product and its warranty status. If Apple doesn't recognize yours, or shows a different model from what you have, that's a strong reason to doubt the AirPods.

A good result isn't the whole story

A serial number that checks out is a strong sign, but it only proves the number is real, not necessarily that the earbuds in your hand came with it. If the price was far below normal or the seller was a stranger online, keep the other warning signs below in mind, and ask an Apple Store to look at them if you're unsure.

Step 3: Look for the warning signs

In our experience, watch for:

  • The price. New AirPods selling far below Apple's price from an unfamiliar seller are a major red flag.
  • The seller. Apple's location finder at locate.apple.com lists places that sell Apple products, including authorized resellers. Marketplace sellers that aren't Apple or a known retailer carry more risk.
  • Mismatched details. Serial numbers that don't match between the case, box and Settings.
  • Quality. Rough edges, loose hinges, blurry printing on the box, or sound that's much worse than you expected.

If you think yours are fake

  1. To be safe, don't leave them charging unattended or overnight until you know they're genuine.
  2. Ask the seller or marketplace for a refund, and report the listing as counterfeit.
  3. If you paid by credit card, contact your card company about disputing the charge.
  4. Report it to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

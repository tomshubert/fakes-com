How to Tell if Your Arc'teryx Is Real

Fake Arc'teryx jackets are common and often well made. Here's what Arc'teryx itself says to check, and where to buy.

By Tom Shubert · Updated Nov 25, 2026

Arc'teryx jackets are expensive, which makes them a favorite of counterfeiters. Arc'teryx says fakes are common and are often made well enough to fool buyers, and that websites claiming to sell genuine Arc'teryx often look real too. The brand doesn't offer a serial number lookup, but it does say where its products are sold and which details to look at. Here's that guidance in plain English.

Where to buy

The safest place is Arc'teryx itself or a store it has authorized. Arc'teryx has a store finder on its own site, and its U.S. online store is at arcteryx.com.

Where you buy also decides whether you get a warranty. Arc'teryx's limited warranty covers only the original buyer with proof of purchase from an authorized Arc'teryx dealer. It specifically leaves out:

  • products bought on third-party auction sites
  • products from unauthorized dealers selling through online marketplaces
  • counterfeit products
  • used products, unless bought through an Arc'teryx used gear platform or program, such as its own ReBIRD resale site

So a bargain from a marketplace seller can cost you the warranty even when the jacket turns out to be genuine.

What Arc'teryx says to look at

Arc'teryx lists four details on its counterfeit help page:

  • Velcro tabs. Genuine tabs are tapered and made of soft, flexible material, without extra decoration or rubbery tabs.
  • Zippers. Arc'teryx says it uses only high-quality YKK or Vislon zippers, with pulls coated to match the color of the garment.
  • Seam tape. On genuine pieces, the tape sealing the seams is narrow, flexible and neatly applied.
  • Hangtags and labels. Every genuine product is labeled and branded, with the labels stitched in place and consistent.

The warranty also excludes products whose hologram or date-code tags have been removed or defaced, so a missing tag is worth asking the seller about.

These checks can't prove a jacket is real

Arc'teryx says counterfeits are often well made, so passing these checks only lowers the odds of a fake. Buying from Arc'teryx or an authorized store is still the best protection.

Warning signs

  • A real-looking website. Arc'teryx warns that fraudulent sites often look authentic. Use Arc'teryx's own store finder to check a seller.
  • Auction and marketplace listings. Arc'teryx says it monitors auction sites and fake websites, and its warranty doesn't cover these purchases.
  • Details that don't match the list above. Rubbery Velcro tabs, unbranded zippers, wide or messy seam tape, or labels that look loose or inconsistent.

If you think you bought a fake

  1. Tell Arc'teryx. It asks anyone who has found or bought a counterfeit to email reportafake@arcteryx.com, and says it may follow up depending on the situation.
  2. Keep your records: the listing, the receipt and any messages with the seller.
  3. Try to sort it out with the seller first. If you paid by credit card, you can dispute the charge with your card company; the FTC says to call and also write to the card company within 60 days of the first bill showing the problem.
  4. If the seller won't fix it, report them to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

U.S. Customs and Border Protection gives similar advice for any brand: shop with retailers and online sellers you can trust. It says most of the fakes it seizes arrive in small packages sent by international mail and express shipping, the way many online orders travel.

Scam Alerts

Updated daily · Week of Nov 9, 2026
Email · TextNov 9

"Unpaid toll" messages are back

The Maine Turnpike Authority warned that E-ZPass customers are getting emails demanding immediate payment for unpaid tolls. The agency says it never sends messages like this.

What to do: Don't click. Log in to your toll account by typing its address yourself.
Source: The Pulse of NH
Phone · BusinessNov 7

Callers pressuring employees to use Bitcoin ATMs

Police in Ocean City, Md., say scammers are calling hotels, praising staff and offering a "bonus" for settling a supposed balance with cash from the register through a Bitcoin ATM.

What to do: Tell staff no real owner or supplier asks for this. Hang up and call the manager directly.
Source: CoastTV
VeteransNov 9

Scams aimed at service members and veterans

New York's attorney general published a guide to pension scams, fake help with VA benefits, phishing, and overpriced "special deals" sold to active-duty troops.

What to do: Get VA benefits help only from VA-accredited representatives. Help is free.
Source: Yahoo News
DonationsOct 16

Fake political donation and "PAC" appeals

The FBI warned that scammers use candidates' names and slogans for fake campaign investment pools and fake political action committees that pocket the money.

What to do: Give through a campaign's official website, never through a link in a text.
Source: The Register

Guides
Deepfakes & AI

Is that really your grandson calling?

AI can copy a voice from a few seconds of video. A short checklist before you send money:

  1. Hang up and call the person back on a number you already have.
  2. Ask a question only they would know.
  3. Agree on a family code word now, before you need it.
Real vs. Fake Products

Buying name brands online without getting a knockoff

Counterfeits show up on big marketplaces too. Signs to check:

  1. A price far below every authorized seller.
  2. A seller name that doesn't match the brand.
  3. Photos copied from the brand's own site.
Fake Websites

How to check a store before you pay

Fake shops can look polished. A quick check takes a minute:

  1. Search the store's name plus "scam" or "reviews".
  2. Look for a real address and phone number.
  3. Pay by credit card, never wire, gift card or crypto.

Scam Alerts

All alerts →
PhoneNov 12

Calls claiming your child was hurt and needs money

Norman, Oklahoma, police say callers are telling parents their child was in a crash or an assault and was injured, then asking for money for medical care or bail. Someone may come on the line pretending to be the child, blaming an injury for sounding different, and the caller may say police took the child's phone. They push for fast payment by gift card, payment app or wire transfer.

What to do: Hang up and call your child yourself, then contact your local police. Never pay with gift cards or give personal details to someone who called you.
Source: KOKH Fox 25, reporting the Norman (Okla.) Police Department warning (Nov 12, 2026) · Norman Transcript, reporting the Norman Police Department warning (Nov 12, 2026)
OnlineNov 12

Illegal online pharmacies selling unsafe medicine

The Georgia Attorney General's Office warns that some online pharmacies are illegal and may sell counterfeit, expired, unapproved or poorly stored drugs that can harm your health. Warning signs include selling prescription drugs without a valid prescription, prices that look too low, and no checkable U.S. address or licensed pharmacist.

What to do: Before ordering, confirm the pharmacy is licensed through your state board of pharmacy and ask a licensed pharmacist if you have questions. Report a suspicious pharmacy to your state attorney general.
Source: WSB-TV via Yahoo News, reporting the Georgia Attorney General's Office alert (Nov 12, 2026)
Social mediaEmailNov 12

Fake charities asking for holiday donations

Graham, North Carolina, police warn that scammers set up fake charities or pose as real ones during the holiday giving season, reaching people by social media, email, text or phone. They use emotional stories, urgent appeals and look-alike fundraising pages, and may ask for gift cards, cryptocurrency or transfers to personal accounts.

What to do: Research a charity with a charity rating service before giving, and donate through its official website rather than a link in a message you did not expect. Report a fake charity to your local police or the FTC.
Source: WXLV ABC45, reporting the Graham (N.C.) Police Department warning (Nov 12, 2026)
OnlineNov 11

Fake rental listings on real estate websites

North Canton, Ohio, police say scammers are posting fake rental listings on well-known real estate websites. Renters are asked to fill out an application with pay stubs and bank records, then to send a security deposit or other payment by bank transfer, which can lead to lost money and identity theft. Police note that a listing on a familiar site is not proof it is real.

What to do: Confirm who owns the property and that the person is allowed to rent it before sharing financial documents or paying any deposit. If you shared information or sent money, contact your local police.
Source: Cleveland19, reporting the North Canton (Ohio) Police Department warning (Nov 11, 2026)

AI videoNov 12Fake

Saluting veteran at a Veterans Day parade

A Facebook video posted days before Veterans Day showed a scarred veteran being saluted at a parade and was shared as real footage. Lead Stories found it was made with AI.

How you can tell: Watch the details: a girl's drumsticks vanish from her hands, the buttons and medals on the man's uniform melt into the fabric, and the flag's stripes change width as it moves. Real cameras don't do that.
Fact check: Lead Stories (Nov 12, 2026)
AI-edited photoNov 2Fake

Sharp photo of the Florida jet crash

After a Learjet crashed in Palm Beach County, Florida, on November 1, a crisp photo spread on X that seemed to show the plane missing a wing. Lead Stories found it was apparently a frame from real dashcam video cleaned up with AI, and the real footage shows both wings.

How you can tell: Be wary of a news photo that looks sharper than any real footage of the event. AI "enhancement" can invent details that weren't there.
Fact check: Lead Stories (Nov 2, 2026)
